About us

Reading help for every age, in one place.

Parents Go To is one place for the reading resources that matter to your family. We gather trusted books, guides and library links for children aged 0 to 18, and sort them by age and by interest, so you are not starting from a search box every time.

Add each child, save what they are reading, and keep a wishlist the whole family can see. Everything stays inside your account until you choose to share it.

One place, not a dozen tabs

Book lists, reading tips and library links sit together in one spot. Every resource is checked by a moderator before it goes up, so you are not the one vetting it.

Matched to your child

Filter by age band and by what they actually like — picture books at three, graphic novels at thirteen. The shelf keeps up as each child grows.

A wishlist the family can see

Keep a running list of the books they want next, then share it with grandparents and family friends so birthday and Christmas gifts land well.

What is included

Everything your family’s reading needs, in one account.

Eight things Parents Go To does for you, from the first picture book to the last year of school. All of it comes with a family account — there is nothing to add on later.

  1. 01 Set up once

    Start with your children

    Every part of the account works from who is actually reading at home.

    • A profile for every child

      Add each child once, with their age band and what they actually like reading. The shelf, the filters and the suggestions all work from that, and they keep up as the child grows.

    • Up next, picked for each child

      Suggestions for the next book come from what is already on that child’s shelf, so a seven-year-old and a teenager never get the same list.

  2. 02 Keep and find

    Build the shelf

    Your own books and the outside resources worth your time, side by side.

    • Your family library

      Save every book in one shelf, sorted by category and age band — picture books and early readers through to non-fiction and graphic novels. A book can belong to one child, to several, or to nobody in particular.

    • Resources worth trusting

      Websites, library programmes and guides for parents, every one of them checked by a moderator before it goes up. Filter by category or by who it suits, and links that leave the site are marked as such.

  3. 03 Day to day

    Keep the reading going

    Record the reading as it happens, including the last few pages before lights out.

    • Reading recorded as it happens

      Log who read what, and for how long. The dashboard keeps the running picture, so you can see what is being read again and again — and what has been sitting untouched on the shelf.

    • A bedtime mode they can use alone

      A calm, dark screen for recording tonight’s reading in three taps: who, which book, how long. There is nothing to wander into, and the way out can be locked with your password.

  4. 04 Beyond your home

    Share it with the family

    Bring in the grandparents, carers and friends who read with your children and buy their books.

    • Wishlists you can share

      Keep a running list of the books they want next, then send a read-only link to grandparents and family friends. They need no account of their own, and you can switch the link off whenever you like.

    • Another adult on the shelf

      Invite a second parent, a grandparent or whoever reads with your children. They see each child’s shelf so they can help, but they cannot change your settings or share anything under your name — and you can take their access away at any time.

How it works

Three steps, and you only do the first one once.

  1. Set up your family profile

    Add your children’s ages, your suburb and anything a provider should know, such as allergies or support needs.

  2. Compare what is near you

    Filter by age, day and distance. Listings sit side by side, so you are comparing like with like.

  3. Book without signing up again

    We pass your details to the provider you chose, and only to them. Your bookings stay in one dashboard.

Privacy

What we collect, and what we do with it.

Parents Go To is used by families, and most of what it holds is about children. This page says plainly what that is. It is written for a parent reading on a phone, not for a lawyer.

Last updated 5 September 2026. Applies to the Parents Go To website and app.

Where your shelf is kept.

Everything you type stays in this browser, on this device, in an area called local storage. Clearing your browser data clears your shelf. Nothing is transmitted to us, and nothing can be recovered by us if you lose it.

What we collect

Two kinds of thing, and nothing else.

About you

  • Your name and email address, so the account has an owner.
  • Your password, so nobody else can open your shelf.
  • Your settings — theme, text size, notification choices.
  • Anything you type into a support report or a resource suggestion.
  • Anything you write on the message board, including the age band you attach to it if you attach one, and whether it is waiting for review, published or removed.
  • Any review you write of a book: the rating out of five, what you wrote, the title and author of the book it is about, and whether it is waiting for review, published or removed. The title and author are kept on the review itself rather than pointed at, so a review does not become unreadable when a book leaves your shelf.
  • The name and email address of any other adult you invite onto your shelf, who invited them, when, and whether their access has since been withdrawn.

If you are not signed in

The contact form on the home page can be used without an account. It keeps the name and email address you type and the message you write, so an administrator can read it and reply. Nothing else is taken — no cookie is set to recognise you, and the message is not linked to any account.

That message is stored in the browser you sent it from and stays on that device. It is not transmitted and we do not receive it.

About your children

  • A first name, or whatever you choose to call them. It does not have to be their real name.
  • An age, if you give one. It is optional.
  • A reading level and their favourite categories, used to filter the shelf.
  • What they are reading, what they have finished, and how far through they are.

We do not ask for a surname, a school, an address, a photograph or a date of birth, and there is nowhere in the app to enter one. We do not collect anything from a child directly — a parent or guardian enters everything, and bedtime mode lets a child log a book without creating a profile of their own.

Why we collect it

The age and reading level exist so the library can show books that suit each child instead of everything at once. Reading progress exists so a half-finished book can be picked back up. Your email exists so you can sign in and get your password back. That is the whole list. We do not build advertising profiles, we do not pass your details to anyone else, and we do not use what is on your shelf to train anything.

How long we keep it

  • Your account and profiles — until you delete them.
  • The reading log — for as long as you have an account, unless you set a shorter period under Settings → Privacy & safety, where you can choose one year or three years.
  • Support reports, contact form messages and resource suggestions — kept while they are open and for a reasonable period after they are closed, so a decision can be explained if you ask about it. The store holds the most recent 200 and drops the oldest beyond that.
  • A shared wishlist — until the expiry you chose when you shared it, or until you press Stop sharing, whichever comes first.
  • Message board posts — kept while the board holds them. A post a moderator removes stops being visible to other parents but is marked removed rather than erased, so the decision and its reason can still be explained. The board holds the most recent 200 posts and drops the oldest beyond that.
  • Other adults you have invited — kept while they are on the shelf, and kept after you withdraw their access, marked withdrawn and dated. A record of who could once see your children that disappears the moment they stop being able to is not a record. This device holds the 12 most recent of these across every account on it, invited and withdrawn together, and drops the oldest beyond that.
  • Book reviews — the same rules as a board post, and the same limit of 200. A review outlives the book it is about: books are held in memory and rebuilt every time the page loads, while a review is written to storage, so deleting a book from your shelf does not delete what you wrote about it. It stays in your own list, marked as being about a book you no longer have.

When you delete your account, all of it goes at once. There is no archive copy.

Who can see it

By default, only you. A child’s name, age and reading level never leave your account unless you choose to share something.

  • Anyone you send a wishlist link to sees the books on that wishlist and the first name of the child it is for — or only their initial, if you ticked “Hide my child’s name”. They cannot see your email, your other children, your library, your reading log or your settings, and they cannot change anything.
  • Other parents see any message you publish on the message board: the name on your account, what you wrote, and the age band you chose if you chose one. They do not see your email, your children, your library or your reading log. A message is only ever seen by other parents after an administrator has published it — until then it is visible to you alone.
  • Other parents who own the same book see any review you publish of it: the name on your account, your rating and what you wrote. They do not see the rest of your shelf, which child it was for, or anything else about your account. As with a message, a review is only ever seen by anyone else after an administrator has published it, and if you change a published review it comes back off the book until it has been looked at again.
  • An administrator sees your account name and email, and anything you send them: a support report, a message from the contact form, a resource you suggest, or a message you write on the board. They cannot open your shelf, and they never see your children’s names, ages or reading history — unless you write one into a box yourself. See below.
  • Another adult you invite onto your shelf would see the most of anyone, which is why the invite dialog lists what before it lists a field. They would see your children by name, with their ages and reading levels, and everything on the shelf and in the reading log. This is the only route in the product by which a child’s name reaches somebody outside your account by design rather than because you typed it into a box. You choose it, you can see who has it on your Profiles tab, and you can withdraw it. There is more about it below.
  • Nobody else. There are no third-party trackers, no analytics scripts and no advertising on this site.

Giving another adult access

Most of this notice is about keeping your children’s details inside your account. Inviting another adult is the one feature that is deliberately about letting them out, so it is worth its own section rather than a line in a list.

A carer — a second parent, a grandparent, whoever reads with your children — sees your children by name, with their ages and reading levels, and everything on the shelf. That is the point of inviting them: somebody who cannot see what a child is reading cannot help them read. But it is a real disclosure, so the invite dialog names the children it is about before it asks you for anything, rather than describing it as “access to your shelf”. A phrase is not consent. A list of names is.

What they do not get is your account. They cannot add or remove a child, change your settings, export your data, share a wishlist, invite anybody else, or post, review or suggest anything under your family’s name. They cannot take your access away; you can take theirs, from the card on your Profiles tab, at any time and without giving a reason.

No invitation email is sent. Nobody is asked to accept one, and there is no separate sign-in for an invited adult. What we store is a name, an email address and the fact that you said this person should have access.

Withdrawing keeps the record. The person moves to a list of people who used to have access, with the date, and stays there until you delete your account. That is deliberate: knowing that somebody once had access, and when it stopped, is part of knowing where your family’s information has been.

Boxes you can type anything into

Four places in this app let you write whatever you like: the contact form on the home page, a support report, the message board, and a review of a book. Everywhere else, the app asks for specific things and keeps them where they belong — a child’s name and age sit inside your account and no administrator has a screen that can show them.

A free-text box is the exception, and the message board is the widest one, because what you write there is read by a moderator and then, once published, by other parents. If you type your child’s name into it, you have told all of them your child’s name. Nothing else in the product does that, and it is worth saying plainly rather than leaving it for you to work out.

So: please do not. The board asks you not to, above the box. It offers an age band instead, which is usually what you meant anyway — “my seven-year-old” rather than a name. A moderator can remove a message for containing information about a child, and that is the first reason on their list.

A book review is the same box in a smaller frame, and it asks the same thing of you, in the same place, above the button. It is worth one extra sentence, because a review is attached to a book rather than floating free: what it can give away is not only a name but a pairing — this child, this book, this age. The review itself never carries a child, and the app does not ask you which one it was for. If you write one in anyway, it goes wherever the review goes.

There is one thing we do not pass on, and it is a decision rather than an oversight. A moderator reading your review is shown the book, your rating and your words. They are not shown the age band of that book, even though the app knows it, because an age band beside your email is a reading level in everything but name, and nothing about publishing a review requires anybody to hand that over.

What we cannot do is stop you. There is no filter reading your message before it is stored and no check on what goes in. The protection here is a request, a prompt and a way of taking something down afterwards — not a control. A message removed by a moderator stops being visible to other parents, but it is marked removed rather than erased, and it stays in this browser’s storage until the site data is cleared.

How to get a copy

Go to Settings → Privacy & safety and choose Download my data. You get everything the app holds about your family as a single file you can keep or move to another device. You do not have to ask us, and you do not have to give a reason.

How to delete it

In the same place, choose Delete my account. That removes your account, every child profile, the library, the wishlist and the reading log. It cannot be undone, so take a copy first if you want one. Individual children can be removed one at a time from the Profiles tab.

Your rights, and how to complain

We follow the Privacy Act 1988 (Cth), the Australian Privacy Principles, and the Children’s Online Privacy Code. Under those you can ask what we hold about you, ask for a copy, ask us to correct something that is wrong, and ask us to delete it.

If you think we have mishandled your family’s information, tell us first — use Settings → Help & support, or the contact form on the home page. We will acknowledge it within 5 working days and give you an answer within 30 days.

If you are not satisfied with our answer, you can complain to the Office of the Australian Information Commissioner. The OAIC is the independent regulator.

Accessibility

Built to be usable by everyone.

A parent might be reading this one-handed at bedtime, on a cracked phone, with a screen reader, or with the lights off. All of those are normal. This page says what we have built for, what we have checked, and what we know is still missing.

Last updated 6 September 2026.

The standard we aim for

Parents Go To targets WCAG 2.2 Level AA — the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines published by the W3C, and the level Australian government services are held to. We believe the site substantially meets it. “Substantially” is doing real work in that sentence: see the known gaps below.

What we have built for

Keyboard

Every control can be reached and operated with a keyboard alone. Tab moves forward, Shift+Tab back, Enter and Space activate. Tab strips use arrow keys. Dialogs trap focus while they are open, Escape closes them, and focus returns to the button that opened them. A “Skip to content” link is the first thing Tab reaches on every page. Nothing is reachable by mouse only.

Focus visibility

Every focusable element has its own visible focus ring, drawn with :focus-visible so it appears for keyboard users without following a mouse around the screen.

Contrast

Text meets at least 4.5:1 against its background, and large text and interface borders meet 3:1. Ratios are calculated from the colour tokens and from the composited background each component actually renders against, in both the light and dark palettes, rather than judged by eye. Colour is never the only way information is given — a status is a word as well as a colour, and an error is text as well as a red outline.

Reduced motion

The shelf animation, panel fades and page transitions all respect prefers-reduced-motion. If your device asks for less motion, the site stops animating. You can also turn motion off inside the app under Settings → Appearance, without changing your device setting.

Forced colours and high contrast

In Windows High Contrast Mode the site drops its own palette and uses your chosen system colours. Borders are drawn so that cards, pills and dialogs keep their shape when the background colour is removed.

Screen readers

Headings are in order and describe the page. Every form control has a real label. Errors are announced when they appear, and confirmations go through a polite live region so they are read without interrupting. Tabs, dialogs and radio groups use the correct roles. Icons that carry no information are hidden from screen readers; icons that do carry information have text beside them.

Text size and zoom

The layout works at 200% zoom without horizontal scrolling. There is also a “Bigger text” switch under Settings → Appearance, which lifts every size across the app.

Touch targets

Interactive controls are at least 44×44 CSS pixels on touch screens, with spacing so a neighbouring control is not hit by mistake.

How it has been tested

Testing so far has been done by the project team, not by an independent auditor:

  • Automated rule scan of every page against WCAG 2.0, 2.1 and 2.2 at levels A and AA, covering form labelling, name and role, heading order, landmark structure, duplicate identifiers and broken label references.
  • Contrast ratios calculated from the design tokens and from the composited background each component renders against, in both palettes.
  • Static review of keyboard structure: focus order, focus indicators, skip-link targets, and tab and dialog semantics.
  • Keyboard-only walkthrough of every screen, including all dialogs.
  • Reduced-motion and forced-colours behaviour checked by toggling the system settings.
  • Screen reader passes over the main flows.

The most recent audit ran on 6 September 2026. It found and fixed four text contrast failures in status labels, eight headings that skipped a level, two skip links whose target could not take focus, and a focus indicator on 111 form controls that depended on a border colour and a shadow — neither of which is painted in forced-colours mode, which would have left keyboard focus invisible there. The automated scan now reports no violations on any page.

Telling us about a barrier

If something stopped you, we want to know — a specific description helps more than a general one. Tell us what you were trying to do, what got in the way, and what you were using: browser, device, and any assistive technology.

  • Signed in: Settings → Help & support, and choose the area it happened in. You get a reference number and can follow the report.
  • Not signed in: use the contact form on the home page. It writes into the same queue a signed-in parent’s report goes into, and you get a reference number back. The contact details beside the form are the quickest route to a person.

We aim to acknowledge a report within 5 working days. If a fix will take longer than that, we will tell you what we are doing instead in the meantime.

Terms of Use

The ground rules, in plain words.

These are the terms you agree to when you create a family account. They are short on purpose. If anything here is unclear, ask us before you sign up.

Last updated 21 September 2026. Applies to the Parents Go To website and app.

Who can have an account

An account is for an adult who is a parent, guardian or carer. Children use the reading space inside that adult’s account; they do not have accounts of their own. You can invite other adults onto your shelf, and they see only what the invitation allows.

Looking after your account

  • Keep your password to yourself. Anything done while signed in to your account is treated as done by you.
  • Give us an email address you can read, so a password reset can reach you.
  • If you think someone else has got in, change your password in Settings → Password & security and sign out everywhere else.

Your children’s details

Add only what you are comfortable with. A first name or a nickname, an age band and the kinds of books they like are all the app needs. The privacy notice says exactly what is kept and who can see it.

What you post

Message board posts, book reviews and resource suggestions are read by a moderator before anyone else sees them. When you post, you agree to:

  • be kind, and write the way you would to another parent at the school gate;
  • leave out children’s full names, schools, photos and anything else that could identify a child;
  • post only what you have the right to share, and nothing that is advertising or spam.

We can decline or remove anything that breaks these rules, and pause an account that keeps breaking them. If you want something you posted taken down, ask through Settings → Help & support and we will remove it.

Links to other sites

Resources and books can link to libraries, publishers and other websites. Links that leave the site are marked. We check a resource before it is listed, but we do not run those sites and are not responsible for what they do.

Your data is yours

You can download everything on your shelf, or delete your account, from Settings → Privacy & safety. Your shelf is kept in the browser on your device, so clearing your browser data also clears it.

Changes to the service and these terms

We may add, change or retire features. If we change these terms, we will update the date at the top of this page and say what changed.

Questions

Signed in, use Settings → Help & support. Not signed in, use the contact form on the home page.

See also: our privacy notice and our accessibility statement.

            Common questions

            Where is my shelf actually stored?

            In this browser, on this device. Nothing is sent to a server. That means it is private by default, and also that clearing your browser data clears the shelf. Keep using the same browser on the same device and it stays put.

            Can I move the shelf to a new phone or laptop?

            Yes. Download a backup on the old device, then open the app on the new one and choose Restore from a backup. Everything comes back exactly as it was.

            My child logged a book on the wrong name. Can I fix it?

            Open the book in the Library, change who it is for, and save. The reading log follows the change, so the totals on each profile update straight away.

            How do I print a reading log for school?

            Your shelf lives in this browser. Keep using the same browser on the same device and everything stays where you left it.

            I forgot my password.

            Sign out, then choose Forgot password on the sign-in screen. If you have a backup contact saved above, that is where the reset goes.

                    What happens when I suspend an account?

                    Sign-in is blocked immediately and the parent sees a message telling them to get in touch. Nothing on their shelf is deleted, and lifting the suspension puts everything back.

                    The shelf lives in their browser, so clearing site data or switching device empties it. Ask whether they have a backup file; if so, Restore from a backup in their account menu brings the shelf back.
