Reading for pleasure

Our first service, and it is open today. Story sessions, reading buddies and book swaps near you, plus a shelf that keeps track of what everyone at home is reading.

Open now

Nothing to prove

There is no reading test and no reading level. A child who wants to read the same dinosaur book eleven weeks running is doing it right, and the shelf will say so.

Create a family account

What is included

Everything your family’s reading needs, in one account.

All of it comes with a family account, and there is nothing to add on later.

  • A profile for every child

    Add each child once, with their age band and what they actually like reading. The shelf, the filters and the suggestions all work from that, and they keep up as the child grows.

  • Your family library

    Save every book in one shelf, sorted by category and age band — picture books and early readers through to non-fiction and graphic novels. A book can belong to one child, to several, or to nobody in particular.

  • Resources worth trusting

    Websites, library programmes and guides for parents, every one of them checked by a moderator before it goes up. Filter by category or by who it suits, and links that leave the site are marked as such.

  • Reading recorded as it happens

    Log who read what, and for how long. The dashboard keeps the running picture, so you can see what is being read again and again — and what has been sitting untouched on the shelf.

  • A bedtime mode they can use alone

    A calm, dark screen for recording tonight’s reading in three taps: who, which book, how long. There is nothing to wander into, and the way out can be locked with your password.

  • Wishlists you can share

    Keep a running list of the books they want next, then send a read-only link to grandparents and family friends. They need no account of their own, and you can switch the link off whenever you like.

Sessions near you

What actually happens at one.

Sessions are run by libraries, bookshops, schools and volunteer groups. They vary, but the listing tells you which kind before you go.

  • Story sessions

    Someone reads aloud to a group, usually 30 to 45 minutes, usually for under-sevens. Drop in, no booking, and nobody minds if a toddler wanders off halfway.

  • Reading buddies

    An older child or an adult volunteer reads alongside one child each week. Good for a reader who has gone quiet, or who would rather not read in front of a class.

  • Book swaps

    Bring books your family has finished, take home books another family has finished. There is no requirement to bring anything to take something.

  • Holiday reading clubs

    Run across school holidays, usually weekly, often with a theme. Listings show the age range and whether siblings can come along to the same session.

Run reading sessions?

Libraries, schools, bookshops and community groups can list their sessions here, so families nearby can find them.
